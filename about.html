Background

Why Gaza Cola?

When I first started thinking about what product to create for this assignment, my initial idea was to model a perfume bottle, gain the creativity points for it not being cans and call it a day. However after thinking about it I decided I wanted to use this project for something more personal and meaningful to me. I am Palestinian, and I wanted to use this assignment as an opportunity to represent my heritage and identity.

As a Palestinian I am aware of the privilege I have in being here, the privilege to study, to learn, the privilege to complain about coursework and projects, opportunities that many do not have. The difference between me and them is geographic luck. In another world, I do not posses this privilege, in another world I am amongst them dreaming of the endless possibilities this world has robbed me of. This project became more than just an assignment. It became a way to represent something I care so deeply about.

Gaza Cola is a real brand created by Palestine House, a Palestinian cultural organisation based in London, where 100% of profits go to humanitarian projects in Palestine. https://gazacola.shop/ and https://palestine-cola.co.uk/

I hope this project speaks to you as much as it does to me. It was so wonderful to do this and i am so grateful to get the chance to showcase this.

What a lovely taste of freedom.

Thank you!!!

Built With

Three.js r160 for the 3D viewer, Bootstrap 5 for the for the layout and responsive grid, custom CSS with a variable system for all brand colours and typography. 3 Google Fonts: Cormorant Garamond for the headings, Inter for the body text, Great Vibes for the Taste of Freedom title.

3D Viewer

The models I made in Blender get loaded into the website as .glb files using Three.js GLTFLoader. The drag and zoom using OrbitControls, the animations using AnimationMixer (which plays them forward to open and backwards to close). The wireframe button swaps the real materials out for MeshBasicMaterial to make sure it shows clean with the lighting. There's 3 lighting moods using DirectionalLights that you can cycle through. The Audio when you open the cans and bottle is using the Web Audio API.

3D Modelling

Built in Blender using Bezier curves and Circle curves with the Screw modifier instead of basic mesh primitives like in the labs. The lid and cap are separate objects so they animate independently. UV unwrapped, textured and keyframe animated in Blender before exporting as individual .glb files

The 3D Models

All 3 models were built in Blender and instead of starting with a basic cylinder mesh like in the labs I used Bezier curves and Circle curves with the Screw modifier and traced the silhouette and used the Screw modifier to spin it 360 degrees to make the 3d cylinder shape. I found it to be much easier than the labs. in the blender screenshots you can see theres 4 models, the one on the edge is the final bottle model that I use for the webapp, you can see the difference in the 2 bottles and how much cleaner the final model is in comparison. (make sure you zoom in when openning the .glb files :) )

The bottle body and cap are two separate objects with the body being Circle.001 and the cap is Circle.004, both made using Circle curves with the the Cap_Open.001 animation stored directly on Circle.004 in the outliner, that is the keyframe animation that plays in the browser when you press Open Cap. The cans work the same way with the body as a BezierCurve object and the lid as separate BezierCurve objects nested inside.

Loading the Models

Once the models were exported from Blender as .glb files I loaded them into the browser using Three.js GLTFLoader with DRACOLoader attached for compression. I added three callbacks one for success, one for progress that updates a live percentage on the loading overlay, and one for errors that triggers the error fallback. I also added a load token system so if you switch products quickly before one finishes loading it cancels and doesnt overwrite the new one. If something goes wrong and the model takes too long, it just shows the fallback automatically instead of leaving the canvas blank. Once the first model loads the other two quietly download in the background so when you switch products it feels instant rather than having to wait.

Camera and Scene

The camera uses a 27 degree field of view which gives it that natural product photography look. A wide angle would make the can look distorted which would look wrong. I applied ACESFilmic tone mapping which is basically a colour grading setting and it makes the lighting look more like realistic instead of flat. The scene has fog and a large backdrop sphere so the background fades out naturally rather than just cutting off.

OrbitControls and Zoom

Drag and zoom are handled by OrbitControls from Three.js. I customised it quite a bit from the defaults. Pan is disabled so the product always stays centred. Vertical rotation is limited so you cannot flip the model upside down. I also customised the zoom to feel smooth rather than harsh and used Damping is enabled on OrbitControls so when you stop scrolling or dragging it slows down gradually instead of stopping quickly, which makes it feel more high quality.

The Can Opening

When you open can the tab gets pulled up which makes the the lid DOWN and inward like a hinge. So I redesigned the animation to replicate this and I named the parts in Blender as Can_Lid and Can_Tab so JavaScript could find them using getObjectByName and move them completely independently from the rest of the can. When Open Cap button is clicked the tab rotates up on the Z axis while the lid pivots inward at the same time, Everything resets back to exactly where it started when you press Reset View. 

const lid = currentProductObject.getObjectByName("Can_Lid");
                   const tab = currentProductObject.getObjectByName("Can_Tab");

                    lid.rotation.z = -0.6;
                    lid.position.x += 0.12;
                    tab.rotation.z = -0.42;
                    tab.position.x += 0.06;

The Animation System

The opening animations were created in Blender using the keyframe timeline and are stored inside the .glb files automatically on export and Three.js reads them using AnimationMixer. I had the animation play forward to open and reversed it to close instead of making two differnet animations by setting the timeScale to negative one and starting from the end of the clip. clampWhenFinished is set to true so the animation stops exactly at the open or closed position instead of looping. isActionPlaying is set to true which to stop from pressing the button again mid animation and pauses the floating idle animation so the two do not conflict with each other. 

// Open
                  activeAction.timeScale = 1;
                  activeAction.play();

                 // Close
                  activeAction.timeScale = -1;
                  activeAction.time = activeClipDuration;
                  activeAction.play();

The Wireframe

I noticed in the https://users.sussex.ac.uk/~dtc27/Coke%20website/CocaCola example the wireframe is so hard to see so I found a way have it be crisp and clear so that lighting doesnt affect it. When each model loads I save all the original materials, and when wireframe is toggled its creating new MeshBasicMaterial instances with wireframe enabled and put it in. I chose to do it because it ignores lighting completely so the lines show up clean so you can properly see the wireframe. keep in mind that the original materials are never changed, just temporarily swapped it out and back in. I had colours for the wirframes with dark red for the cans and grey for the white bottle. What I like about it is that it is showing the real geometry from Blender, the actual rings the Screw modifier generated which is pretty cool. 

child.material = enabled
                    ? child.userData.meshWireMaterials
                    : child.userData.originalMaterials;

The Lighting System

I set up five lights in the Three.js scene: an ambient light, a hemisphere light, a key directional light as the main source and casts shadows, a fill light on the opposite side softens the shadows, a rim light from behind so the product stands out from the background. I then added three lighting presets that cycle when the Studio Light button is clicked. Default is balanced neutral white, Studio is brighter and more professional, Sunset uses warm orange and golden tones with the key light from a low side angle for golden hour. The button text updates to show the current preset and Reset View always returns to Default.

The Audio

Opening the can or bottle plays a sound using the browser's built in Web Audio API instead of three.audiolistener like the labs. The audio loads in the background when the page first opens using fetch and decodeAudioData so that it is ready before the user presses the button. The can and bottle have different sound files because they sound different in real life. (i got the sounds from youtube and coverted it to mp3, this is for the bottle https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nXXMFDlTu_c and this audio for the cans https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EPkYVHuXY8A ) Each product has a soundType set to either can or bottle and the correct sound plays automatically. The code also checks if the AudioContext is suspended (which browsers do until the user interacts with the page) and resumes it before playing. 

audioContext = new (window.AudioContext || window.webkitAudioContext)();

                    const source = audioContext.createBufferSource();
                    source.buffer = buffer;
                    source.connect(audioContext.destination);
                    source.start(0);

The Shadow and Background

Instead of relying solely on Three.js shadow mapping I created a soft gradient shadow using the HTML Canvas API and placed it as a flat disc under the model. It fades from dark in the centre to transparent in the edges. The canvas background uses a radial gradient, lighter in the centre where the models sit.

The Floating Animation

When the model is just sitting there doing nothing it gently bobs up and down. I did this using a sine wave on the Y position every frame. It pauses automatically when auto rotate is on or when the opening animation is playing so nothing clashes. 

model.position.y = floatingBaseY + Math.sin(elapsed * 1.35) * 0.008;

Memory and Performance

Every time you switch product the old model is fully disposed from memory so the browser does not slow down over time. When you switch to another browser tab the animation clock pauses to save CPU and battery and then restarts when you come back. Event listeners handle WebGL context loss which can happen on mobile when the browser reclaims GPU memory, showing a message instead of silently breaking.

Design Decisions

I used 3 Google Fonts: Cormorant Garamond for headings, Inter for body text, Great Vibes for the Taste of Freedom title because its similar to the Gaza Cola website. Displayed the header in both English Cola Gaza and Arabic غزة كولا. The brand name links back to the homepage following the standard web convention users expect (when you click on the title in about page it redirects you to the home page). The hamburger menu opens a Bootstrap offcanvas panel keeping the header clean across all screen sizes. In the homepage, the product image lifts on hover. Five buttons each use a different style, solid red for main actions, outline red for toggles and soft beige for resetguiding the user toward the most interesting interactions first. The olive branch sits in the homepage hero because the olive tree is one of the most recognised symbols of Palestinian identity and culture. The layout throughout the site uses Bootstrap 5's grid system for example the 4 Blender screenshots use col-lg-3 col-md-6 which means 4 cards sit side by side on desktop, 2 per row on tablet and 1 per row on mobile. Bootstrap handles all the responsive behaviour automatically and I then applied my own custom CSS classes on top for the card styling, colours and typography.

Deeper Understanding

Bezier curve and Screw modifier modelling instead of basic mesh. Web Audio API sound system instead of Three.js's built in audio system (THREE.AudioListener). 15 second timeout fallback. For the wireframe I swapped the materials out for MeshBasicMaterial instead of just setting wireframe true on the existing ones because MeshBasicMaterial ignores lighting so the lines always show up clean and crisp. Trigonometric automatic camera framing per product. Model prefetching for instant switching. WebGL context loss recovery. Full GPU memory disposal on product switch. AnimationMixer reverse playback using timeScale negative one. Damping is enabled on OrbitControls so when you stop dragging or scrolling it slows down gradually which makes the whole interaction feel much smoother. HTML Canvas API procedural soft shadow. ACESFilmic tone mapping. Floating sine wave idle animation. Three lighting presets cycling through Default, Studio and Sunset.

Testing

Testing

Using the testing powerpoint on canvas, I conducted the following tests:

Functional Testing

I tested every interactive element to ensure it worked as intended. Open Cap, Wireframe, Auto Rotate, Studio Light and Reset View were all tested on the models. The close lid / close cap is a bit slow because its in reverse but it functions properly. The product dropdown correctly switches between all three products and updates the URL. The menu opens and closes correctly on all pages. All navigation links were tested and go to the correct pages.

Responsiveness Testing

Using Chrome DevTools I tested the layout at 375px mobile, 768px tablet and 1440px desktop. The two column viewer layout collapsed to single column on smaller screens. The control buttons are laid out correctly. The home and about pages displayed correctly with the text, images and cards all resizing. I made sure that the model page removes the image for the mobile size (375px) because in the mobile screen you want to be able to press the buttons and see the models at the same time, not press the button and quickly scroll up to see the animation because then the users will miss the animation completely. Cross Platform Testing

The site was tested on macOS on a laptop and on an iPhone connecting to the same WiFi and accessing the local server via the IP address and the webapp resized properly to the size of the phone.

Usability Testing

I shared the site with my roomates. They thought the webapp was very pleasing to the eye and cool with the 3d models. They're interior architecture students and are familiar with 3d models / rendering and loved the way my models looked realistic .They also loved the concept of incorperating my heritiage and applauded my creativity. They were surprised at the audio the models had and said " oh my god how did you do that !!!".

Visual Testing

The brand colours, typography and card layouts were consistent across all pages. The colours were visually pleasing and the photos incorporated further tied everything together. The colour scheme is meant to be soft and not too much due to the patterns and pictures already there. It was important to make the colour scheme also relevant to palestine, the colour scheme as you notice has slight incorporations of the colours of the flag like the green for the olive branch , red for buttons and title, black text and soft white bakcground, yet making sure not to overdo it to keep the simple clean accessible look.

Accessibility Testing

Model status label updates automatically so users can see when its loading. I also made sure all text is readable with the colour choices and not harsh on the eyes and to not overwhelm the page with many pictures when the testing screenshots began, so that its easily reeadable and not overstimulating for the users when theres too much going on. Kept the home page to the point and not too overwhelming so that users can clearly understand and click the buttons to view the models.
